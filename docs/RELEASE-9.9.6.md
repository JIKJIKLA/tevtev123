# Championship lighting 9.9.6

This update follows the owner's request for Rocket League's colorful, stylized stadium lighting while preserving matte cars. Reference comparisons use the supplied daytime DFH gameplay screenshot and the [official DFH Stadium arena image](https://cms-assets.unrealengine.com/cmkr1i7c9047e07n0es5291ez/cmdsio9uskt7x07oft345nqxh) from [Rocket League's Season 19 article](https://www.rocketleague.com/news/celebrate-10-years-of-aerials-and-epic-saves-in-rocket-league-season-19?lang=en). These are research references; the images are not bundled into the game. The original approved photographic sky remains.

The light rig now uses a warmer, higher sun; broad neutral/cool diffuse fill; and a warm rear edge. The static environment contributes less overall brightness, its turf bounce is less mint-colored, and bright LED bands retain HDR color at a lower peak. The key/fill balance exposes body shape without adding clearcoat or making the paint glossier. Paint keeps roughness 0.72, metalness 0.04 and zero clearcoat; driver colors and team-color rules are unchanged.

The pitch uses deeper yellow-green mowing panels and a soft baked canopy shade region. Goal backings are lighter matte steel so they do not read as black holes. Soft team-colored frame halos and faint distant lamp shafts are two small shared-geometry batches per effect, rather than a whole-screen glow filter. The goal-side haze is weaker so it does not wash out the field. Grass contact shading uses the car/ball proximity already computed for blade bending; it is bounded and runs in both WebGPU and WebGL, with no extra textures or car-shadow rendering pass.

The existing light budget stays at six lights in Chromebook mode and ten in Detail, with only the Detail sun casting a shadow map. That map stays 2048 square, with tighter arena coverage and a wider filter radius. The pinned Three.js 186 renderer uses the supported PCFShadowMap filter on both backends; the removed PCFSoftShadowMap name is not used. See the [official migration guide](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/wiki/Migration-Guide). No added screen-space pass, scene capture, transmission, hardware ray tracing, resolution reduction, physics change, or new sound is involved.

Visual checks use the actual renderer with held developer poses, the same cameras/materials, and no lighting overrides. The before build is 9.9.5. Checks include the chase camera, garage, both field ends, automatic WebGPU Chromebook quality and explicit WebGL Detail. Developer review output is separate from production and never published in the portable game. Automated coverage includes light/shadow resource bounds, team-colored sources, finite halo geometry, HDR orientation/energy, matte paint, grass materials and all existing game-mode/lifecycle checks.
