# Core match effects catalogue — Nivet League 10.1.0

This catalogue turns the request to recreate normal Rocket League match feedback into auditable effect families. It covers ordinary car soccer, plus Nivet's existing Heatseeker playlist. It is not an exhaustive inventory of Rocket League's proprietary renderer, sound variations, cosmetic items or every arena. Similarity depends on event timing, physical response, camera stability, sound and restrained visual feedback working together; adding particles alone does not reproduce its feel.

The references are inspected for behavior and measurements. No new Rocket League artwork, particle textures or sound recordings were copied for this update. Existing supplied recordings and assets remain. New feedback uses independent geometry and the existing effect maps.

## Reference distinctions that matter

- **Boost is separate from supersonic wheel trails.** The official [v1.35 Anniversary Update](https://www.rocketleague.com/news/patch-notes-v1-35-anniversary-update) introduces Engine Audio, Goal Explosions and Trails as distinct customization categories and places Trails behind the wheels at supersonic speed. Nivet therefore uses separate conditions for exhaust thrust and wheel ribbons.
- **A released effect must not remain stuck on.** The official [v1.45 patch notes](https://www.rocketleague.com/news/patch-notes-v1-45) correct boost sound/visuals continuing after deactivation and address early-match loading stutter. Nivet stops producing thrust when simulated boosting stops, lets already-emitted particles expire, and prepares materials before revealing gameplay.
- **Physical triggers come first.** [RLBot's useful game values](https://wiki.rlbot.org/v5/botmaking/useful-game-values/) documents 22 m/s supersonic entry, 23 m/s maximum car speed, 33.3 boost units per second, and the standard small/large pad behavior. The pinned [RocketSim constants](https://github.com/ZealanL/RocketSim/blob/c2baacb8f4b441dd8505e63c2aeb5a1679b60b02/src/RLConst.h) distinguish the 0.1-second minimum boost burst, supersonic maintenance and three-second demolition respawn. Its [arena contact implementation](https://github.com/ZealanL/RocketSim/blob/c2baacb8f4b441dd8505e63c2aeb5a1679b60b02/src/Sim/Arena/Arena.cpp) provides a primary implementation reference for bumper contact, direction and team exclusions. These are independent community simulation references, not access to Psyonix's engine.

## Vehicle, contact and ball feedback

“Implemented” means the stated behavior exists in current source. “Approximation” means the family is represented with an independently tuned rule or cheaper presentation. A named test suite covers the specified invariant; it does not certify a frame-perfect visual match.

Idle tailpipe smoke has no separate simulation. |

Heatseeker reuses these families while authoritative touch/target state controls its homing and speed changes. The catalogue does not imply support for Rumble powerups, Dropshot tiles, Hoops, Snow Day, weather-specific arena effects or licensed cosmetic variants absent from Nivet.

## Custom opening, separate from normal Rocket League effects

The requested three-jet/ball-drop opening is Nivet's own first-VS-AI-match presentation. It is not listed as a normal Rocket League match effect. The orange leader flies straight while the blue wingmen bank apart; the supplied full-size models fly at 88 m rather than being enlarged near the roof. The stadium remains fixed. Only the camera/HUD get a brief bounded wake, plus 14 tiny dust pieces beneath the canopy. The wall-impact recording was removed from the flyover so it no longer sounds like a roof strike.

The independent ball mesh falls through a fully open fixed roof hatch, bounces and settles at countdown zero. Its rendering never drives the staged physics ball through the collision ceiling. Flyby and match-cinema tests check smooth paths, every aircraft corner's roof clearance, the entire ball's hatch clearance, dust limits, quiet mode and the exact first-match eligibility rules. Online/practice/reduced-motion sessions retain the standard kickoff.

## Cost and correctness guardrails

Ordinary feedback uses fixed pools: one ribbon buffer, 18 short rings, 16 smoke entries and 96 skid entries. Existing boost/goal and pad effects also reuse bounded pools. New contact effects do not allocate a light per event or add a mandatory postprocess pass. Quiet/reduced-motion choices suppress extra motion while retaining essential messages.

Effects never award a hit, goal, save, pickup or demolition merely because an animation played. Shared physical events and server state decide the result. The catalogue identifies remaining gaps openly; matching every authored asset or achieving frame-perfect 1:1 handling requires further measured comparison and playtesting.
